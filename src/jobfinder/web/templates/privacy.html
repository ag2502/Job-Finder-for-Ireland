Your CV file is never stored.

When you add your CV to your profile, we read it once and throw the file away before the page has even finished loading. The document is never written to a disk or a database. What we keep is the short reading of it that ranks your searches.

What is kept from your CV

Only what matching needs, saved with your account so you add your CV once rather than on every search:

  • Skill keywords found in it, such as python, kubernetes and so on
  • The kinds of work it points to, a seniority guess and a rough number of years
  • A one line summary of what you do, when one could be written
  • The file's name and size, and the date you added it, so you can tell which CV it is

The text of your CV is not among them: no address, phone number or email address from it, and no employment history beyond those keywords. Your profile shows everything that was kept, exactly as it is stored.

Remove on your profile deletes all of it outright, and Replace swaps it for the reading of a newer CV.

What else an account keeps

An account is optional. Searching and seeing every job work without one; ranking against your CV, saving jobs and tracking what you applied to need one. It keeps, on a server:

  • Your email address, held by Supabase in the EU. If you sign in with Google, also the name and profile picture Google shares; your Google password never reaches this site. If you use an email and password instead, only a hash of the password is kept.
  • The reading of your CV described above, if you add one.
  • What you told us you are looking for: the kinds of work, years of experience and the remote, internship and graduate choices, if you save them.
  • For each job you mark as applied or save: its title, employer, link and the date.

That is the whole of it: no tracking, no analytics tied to you, nothing shared with employers or anyone else. You can remove any of it from your profile at any time, and removing something deletes the row outright rather than archiving it.

Why it is built this way

A CV is personal data. Under GDPR, storing one makes the operator a data controller with retention, access and erasure duties, and it becomes the most sensitive thing in the system, and the part worth attacking. Keeping the few kilobytes that matching actually uses and discarding the document removes most of that risk, and costs you nothing in results quality.

The job records themselves

Job adverts are public business information, gathered from companies' own careers pages. Every one links back to the company's original posting, which is where you apply. This site never takes an application itself.
