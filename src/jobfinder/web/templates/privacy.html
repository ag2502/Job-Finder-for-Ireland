What happens to your CV

Your CV is never stored. It is read in memory, and the file is discarded before the page loads. Nothing is written to disk or to a database.

What is kept

Only what matching needs, held in a signed cookie in your own browser:

  • Skill keywords found in your CV (e.g. python, kubernetes)
  • A seniority guess and rough years of experience
  • The fields you chose

The text of your CV is not among them. Earlier versions kept a short excerpt for keyword scoring; it turned out to change no results, and it carried whatever the document said — including your email address — so it is gone.

Clearing your session with Start over, or clearing your browser cookies, removes all of it.

If you create an account

An account is optional. Searching, ranking and applying all work without one, and nothing about your CV changes if you have one — it is still never stored.

An account exists to answer one question: have I already applied to this? So it keeps, on a server:

  • Your email address and a password hash, held by Supabase in the EU. Your password itself is never seen by this site.
  • For each job you mark as applied: its title, employer, link and the date.

That is the whole of it — no tracking, no analytics tied to you, nothing shared with employers or anyone else. You can remove any application from the Applications page at any time, and removing one deletes the row outright rather than archiving it.

Why it is built this way

A CV is personal data. Under GDPR, storing one makes the operator a data controller with retention, access and erasure duties, and it becomes the most sensitive thing in the system — the part worth attacking. Extracting the few hundred bytes that matching actually uses and discarding the document removes that risk entirely, and costs you nothing in results quality.

Job listings

Job postings are public business information collected from employers' own careers systems. Each listing links back to the employer's original posting, which is where you apply.
